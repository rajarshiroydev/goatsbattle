# GOATSBattle — Scalable Product Direction

## 1. Product Vision

GOATSBattle should not be built as a narrow “GOAT voting app.”

It should become a structured discussion platform for fandom, starting with football and eventually expanding into other sports and cultural domains such as film, music, literature, gaming, and other areas where people debate greatness.

The long-term vision is:

> Build the definitive discussion layer for entities, events, and moments people care deeply about.

GOAT debates are the initial hook and signature mechanic. They are not the entire product.

The platform should combine:

- Reddit-style threaded discussion
- Discord-style identity, customization, roles, and community belonging
- Structured data around players, matches, clubs, moments, statistics, and rankings
- Persistent debate history instead of disposable chat
- Voting, comparison, reputation, and progression systems

The product should be positioned as a place where discussion is attached to structured real-world objects rather than placed inside a generic feed.

---

## 2. Core Product Thesis

Most online discussion currently lives in flat, general-purpose environments:

- Reddit threads
- Discord channels
- X replies
- YouTube comments
- Instagram comments
- WhatsApp and Telegram groups

These platforms are good at hosting conversation, but they are not designed around the structure of the subject being discussed.

GOATSBattle should organize conversation around entities such as:

- Matches
- Players
- Clubs
- Competitions
- Goals
- Cards
- Refereeing decisions
- Tactical moments
- Rankings
- Battles
- Films
- Directors
- Actors
- Books
- Authors
- Artists
- Albums

Every important object should have its own persistent page, data, history, discussions, polls, rankings, and relationships.

This makes GOATSBattle an entity-centric social platform rather than a generic social network.

---

## 3. Initial Market Focus

The product should begin with football.

Football is the strongest beachhead because it has:

- Global reach
- Frequent live events
- Strong fan identity
- Constant controversy
- Daily news and discussion
- Historic GOAT debates
- Natural rivalry between clubs, players, countries, and generations

The short-term goal is not to become the home for every possible category.

The short-term goal is:

> Become the best place on the internet to discuss football matches, players, moments, and greatness.

Only after strong product-market fit in football should the same engine be expanded into other sports and cultural categories.

---

## 4. Product Layers

GOATSBattle should have two connected layers.

### 4.1 Evergreen Layer

This contains content that remains relevant over time.

Examples:

- Messi vs Ronaldo
- Greatest footballers
- Best managers
- Best World Cup performances
- Player profiles
- Career statistics
- Historic matches
- Rankings over time
- Champion Mode
- All-time debates

This layer gives the brand its identity and creates durable, searchable content.

### 4.2 Fresh Layer

This contains content that changes daily.

Examples:

- Live matches
- Upcoming fixtures
- Post-match discussions
- Refereeing controversies
- Transfer debates
- Tactical debates
- Player-of-the-match voting
- Team selection discussions
- Breaking football topics
- Trending fan arguments

This layer creates daily retention.

The product needs both.

GOAT debates attract and define the brand. Live football discussion gives users a reason to return every day.

---

## 5. Match Coverage Strategy

GOATSBattle should not limit match pages to matches involving selected GOAT contenders.

It should house all relevant football matches within the competitions it supports.

A football fan may not care about an all-time debate every day, but may care deeply about:

- Their club’s next match
- A local league fixture
- A Champions League match
- A World Cup match
- A controversial refereeing decision
- A transfer involving their team
- A breakout performance by a non-GOAT player

Every supported match should have a dedicated discussion experience.

A match page should contain:

- Basic match information
- Timeline of important events
- Goals, cards, substitutions, VAR incidents, and major moments
- Match statistics where useful
- Automatically generated or editorially seeded debate topics
- Threaded discussion
- Polls
- Player-of-the-match voting
- Club and player context
- Links into related GOAT debates and rankings

The match page should not try to beat FotMob or SofaScore as a pure score-and-stat product.

Its purpose is:

> Use match data to create, organize, and enrich conversation.

Every piece of data should exist because it helps users form, support, or challenge an opinion.

---

## 6. Discussion Model

The product should avoid a single unstructured comment stream.

Discussion should be divided into meaningful surfaces.

Examples for a match:

- Main match discussion
- Was the penalty correct?
- Did the manager make the right substitution?
- Who was the best player?
- Was the red card deserved?
- Did one player outperform another?
- What changed the match?
- Tactical discussion
- Fan reactions by club or country

Each debate should be:

- Threaded
- Persistent
- Searchable
- Shareable
- Connected to the relevant entity or moment
- Ranked by usefulness or community response
- Supported by stats, clips, timestamps, or event context when available

Discord-style live chat can exist later, but it should not replace persistent structured discussion.

The product’s advantage is that conversations remain useful after the moment has passed.

## 7. Identity and Community

Users should build a visible identity over time.

A profile should communicate:

- Supported clubs
- National team
- Chosen GOATs
- Favourite players
- Favourite categories
- Reputation
- Debate history
- Voting history
- Badges
- Roles
- Rankings
- Streaks
- Community recognition

Possible identity markers:

- Barcelona supporter
- Messi supporter
- Premier League analyst
- World Cup historian
- Top 1% football debater
- Tactical contributor
- Reliable source badge
- Early community member
- Match-thread regular
- Club-specific role

The platform should borrow Discord’s strongest social property:

> Users feel that they belong somewhere and that their identity is visible.

However, identity should be attached to the wider platform, not trapped inside isolated servers.

---

## 8. Reputation and Progression

Users need reasons to care about their account.

Reputation should reward useful participation, not simply volume.

Possible inputs:

- Upvotes or endorsements
- Accepted factual corrections
- Strong predictions
- High-quality debate contributions
- Community moderation
- Participation in match discussions
- Consistent voting
- Accurate stat references
- Helpful context
- Constructive disagreement

Avoid reducing debates to a simplistic “winner” system based only on popularity.

A strong reputation system should distinguish between:

- Popularity
- Expertise
- Reliability
- Participation
- Sportsmanship
- Category knowledge

Progression features can include:

- Badges
- Levels
- Category-specific rankings
- Club leaderboards
- Seasonal leaderboards
- Match-day streaks
- Debate achievements
- Champion Mode records

These systems create long-term account investment and switching costs.

---

## 9. GOAT Curation Strategy

GOATSBattle should not attempt to catalogue every person in every category at launch.

Each arena should begin with a curated pool of approximately 50 credible contenders.

Examples:

- Top footballers
- Top cricket players
- Top Formula 1 drivers
- Top tennis players
- Top directors
- Top authors
- Top musicians

The limited pool should be presented as intentional curation rather than incomplete coverage.

Possible framing:

> The GOAT Pool contains the most credible contenders in each arena and is updated as careers, achievements, and public opinion evolve.

Selection should eventually combine:

- Editorial judgment
- Transparent inclusion criteria
- Community nominations
- Voting thresholds
- Periodic reviews
- Regional and era diversity

The platform must avoid becoming Wikipedia or IMDb.

Every person included should be genuinely “GOAT-worthy” within the relevant category.

---

## 10. Rankings and the Opinion Graph

Votes should create more than a static leaderboard.

The platform should build a living opinion graph.

It should be possible to understand:

- Who users believe is greatest
- How one contender performs against another
- How opinion changes over time
- How opinion differs by region
- How opinion differs by fanbase
- How opinion changes after major events
- Which achievements influence rankings
- Which match moments change perception
- Which generations vote differently

This becomes more defensible as usage grows.

The long-term value is not merely the number of votes.

It is the structured relationship between:

- Users
- Contenders
- Categories
- Matchups
- Moments
- Regions
- Time periods
- Arguments
- Statistics
- Outcomes

The product should preserve historical snapshots so users can see how opinion evolved.

## 11. Information Architecture

A scalable football structure may look like this:

```text
Football
├── Live
├── Matches
│   ├── Upcoming
│   ├── Live
│   └── Archive
├── Competitions
├── Clubs
├── Players
├── Rankings
├── GOAT Battles
├── Champion Mode
├── Trending Debates
└── The Floor
```

Entity relationships should be reusable.

Example:

```text
Competition
  └── Match
       ├── Team
       ├── Player
       ├── Match Event
       ├── Debate
       ├── Poll
       ├── Comment
       └── Statistic
```

This model should later support other domains.

Example for film:

```text
Film
├── Director
├── Actor
├── Award
├── Scene
├── Review
├── Debate
├── Poll
└── Ranking
```

The underlying platform should be generic enough to support multiple entity types, while the launch product remains deeply optimized for football.

## 12. Retention Loops

Users should return for three reasons.

### 12.1 Fresh Content

- Live matches
- New debate topics
- Transfers
- Controversies
- Rankings movement
- New polls
- Trending discussions

### 12.2 Social Pull

- Replies
- Mentions
- Follows
- Challenges
- Reactions
- Club activity
- Rival fan responses
- Notifications about debates the user joined

### 12.3 Personal Progression

- Reputation
- Badges
- Streaks
- Rankings
- Debate records
- Champion Mode history
- Club or category status

A healthy retention loop combines all three.

Example:

1. A match begins.
2. The user joins a live discussion.
3. The user votes in a controversy poll.
4. Another fan replies.
5. The user receives a notification.
6. Their contribution gains reputation.
7. The debate affects a player or topic ranking.
8. The user returns to see the result.

---

## 13. Launch Strategy

The launch should remain narrow.

Recommended launch scope:

- Football only
- A selected set of major competitions
- Full match coverage within those competitions
- Strong GOAT profiles and battles
- Match pages with structured debates
- Threaded comments
- Identity and fan tags
- Basic reputation
- Shareable results
- Notifications

Avoid launching all sports and cultural categories at once.

The first goal should be to prove:

- Users participate in match discussions
- Users return for later fixtures
- Users create quality debate
- Users respond to notifications
- Users care about identity and reputation
- GOAT content successfully pulls users into wider football discussion
- Match discussion successfully pulls users into GOAT content

---

## 14. Web First, Mobile Later

Launching as a web app is reasonable.

The web product should prioritize:

- Fast loading
- Search-engine discoverability
- Shareable URLs
- Strong desktop and mobile-web usability
- Deep links to specific debates and match moments
- Public pages that can rank in search
- Low-friction onboarding
- Read access before account creation
- Login only when a user votes, comments, follows, or personalizes

Mobile apps should follow once the product proves:

- Repeat usage
- Notification value
- Live-match engagement
- Strong retention
- Clear mobile-specific use cases

Native mobile will become especially valuable for:

- Push notifications
- Live match alerts
- Replies and mentions
- Real-time discussion
- Personalised feeds
- Faster repeat sessions

Do not build native apps merely for credibility. Build them when the web product demonstrates recurring behaviour worth notifying users about.

## 15. Scalability Principles

### 15.1 Separate Core Platform Logic From Category Logic

The platform should have reusable primitives:

- Entity
- Event
- Debate
- Poll
- Vote
- Comment
- Ranking
- Relationship
- User role
- Reputation
- Badge
- Notification

Football-specific features should sit on top of those primitives.

This makes later expansion easier without making the football product generic or weak.

### 15.2 Event-Driven Data Model

Major real-world events should be capable of triggering product actions.

Examples:

- Goal scored
- Red card
- Match begins
- Match ends
- Player transferred
- Award won
- Ranking changes
- New debate created
- User mentioned
- Poll closes

This will support notifications, live updates, trending logic, and automated debate generation.

### 15.3 Avoid Premature Real-Time Complexity

At launch, not everything needs WebSockets or live streaming.

Prioritize real-time infrastructure only for:

- Live match status
- New comments where immediacy matters
- Notifications
- Poll updates
- High-traffic event pages

Other pages can use caching, incremental refresh, server rendering, and background processing.

### 15.4 Build for Sparse Early Communities

The product must feel useful with low user numbers.

Use:

- Seeded debate topics
- Rich entity pages
- Match timelines
- Polls
- Editorial prompts
- Historical discussions
- Trending modules
- Smart defaults
- Strong onboarding into active topics

Do not rely on users to create all structure.

### 15.5 Preserve Data History

Rankings, votes, edits, moderation actions, and opinion changes should be historically traceable.

The historical record may become one of the product’s most valuable assets.

## 16. Content and Moderation

Structured debate can easily become hostility.

The platform should encourage argument without encouraging abuse.

Moderation systems should support:

- Clear community rules
- Reporting
- Rate limits
- Spam protection
- Toxicity controls
- Topic locks
- Moderator roles
- Club and category moderators
- Escalation paths
- Transparent enforcement
- Reputation penalties
- Fact-check or source requests

Politics should not be an early category.

Political discussion has much higher moderation, misinformation, legal, and safety risk than sports, film, music, or literature.

Expansion should first focus on categories where disagreement is passionate but less likely to destabilize the whole community.

---

## 17. Metrics That Matter

Do not optimize only for registrations or total votes.

The most important early metrics are:

- Daily active users
- Weekly active users
- D1, D7, and D30 retention
- Percentage of users who comment
- Percentage of users who vote
- Replies per discussion
- Return rate after receiving a reply
- Match-page engagement
- Percentage of match users who enter GOAT content
- Percentage of GOAT users who enter match content
- Number of active contributors per match
- Time to first meaningful interaction
- Percentage of discussions with multiple participants
- Notification open rate
- Contribution quality
- Reports and moderation burden
- Active users per supported competition

The key question is:

> Does the product create recurring community behaviour around football events?

---

## 18. Product Positioning

Avoid positioning the product as:

- Another social network
- Reddit for sports
- Discord for sports
- A sports statistics app
- A score app
- A generic debate app

Better positioning:

> GOATSBattle is the structured discussion layer for fandom.

Football-specific version:

> GOATSBattle is where football fans debate every match, player, moment, and claim with the context of the game built in.

Broader vision:

> Every important person, event, and moment deserves a living page where the world can debate its significance.

---

## 19. Strategic Guardrails

The following rules should guide product decisions.

1. GOAT debates are the wedge, not the entire product.
2. Football is the initial market, not one of many equal launch categories.
3. All relevant matches in supported competitions should have discussion pages.
4. Data should support discussion rather than compete with specialist score apps.
5. Persistent structured debate is more important than disposable chat.
6. Identity, reputation, and belonging are essential for retention.
7. The product should feel useful before it has a large community.
8. Expansion should happen only after football shows strong retention.
9. Every new category should reuse the same platform primitives.
10. Do not become an uncurated database of everyone and everything.
11. Do not launch politics early.
12. Preserve the history of public opinion over time.
13. Native mobile should follow demonstrated recurring behaviour.
14. Focus on owning one behaviour before competing with general-purpose platforms.
15. The strongest goal is not to “beat Discord” or “beat Reddit.”

The strongest goal is:

> Become the default place for structured football discussion, then expand the model into other forms of fandom.

## 21. Final Direction

GOATSBattle should be built as a scalable platform for structured fandom.

Its signature is the GOAT battle.

Its daily engine is live and ongoing discussion.

Its retention layer is identity, reputation, and community.

Its moat is the growing graph of opinions, debates, entities, events, moments, and historical changes in public belief.

Its initial focus is football.

Its long-term opportunity is much larger:

> A universal system for debating greatness, significance, and performance across the people, events, and works that shape culture.
